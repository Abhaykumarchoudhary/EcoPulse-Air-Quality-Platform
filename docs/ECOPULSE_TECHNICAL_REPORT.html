1. Problem Formulation & Executive Summary

Air pollution monitoring in the National Capital Region (Delhi-NCR) faces fundamental physical and spatial constraints: Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Stations (CAAQMS) provide accurate ground-level measurements but are geographically sparse. Conversely, NASA MODIS satellite instruments yield continuous Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD) observations, but measure column-integrated aerosol extinction rather than surface breathable PM₂.₅, while suffering from severe winter cloud and haze occlusion.

EcoPulse resolves this disconnect through an end-to-end geospatial machine learning architecture that integrates satellite AOD, 16,480 hourly ground readings, and convective meteorology to generate a validated, continuous 1 km daily PM₂.₅ surface grid across Delhi-NCR with real-time gap-filling, anomaly detection, and carbon intelligence.

2. Mathematical & Physical Formulation

Ground-level PM₂.₅ concentration is related to columnar AOD through boundary-layer dynamics and relative humidity hygroscopic swelling:

Columnar-to-Surface Physical Equation:
PM₂.₅ = (AOD / PBLH) · [1 / f(RH)] · (ρ_dry / η_ext)
where PBLH represents Planetary Boundary Layer Height, and f(RH) = (1 - RH)^(-γ) accounts for aerosol hygroscopic growth.

Horizontal dispersion is computed via orthogonal vector decomposition of 10-meter wind fields:

  • U = -WindSpeed · sin(WindDirection) (Zonal advection)
  • V = -WindSpeed · cos(WindDirection) (Meridional advection)

5. Explainability & Feature Importances (TreeSHAP)

  1. Satellite AOD (42.8%): Dominant driver of regional mass aerosol loading.
  2. Boundary-Layer Height (24.1%): Primary physical catalyst of winter pollution spikes via nocturnal thermal inversions.
  3. Relative Humidity (12.4%): Secondary aerosol formation and hygroscopic growth.
  4. Wind Vectors U, V (9.2%): Regional advective dispersion.
  5. Cyclical Diurnal & Seasonal Timing (5.9%): Morning and evening vehicular traffic waves.
  6. Ambient Temperature (5.6%): Seasonal inversion gradients.

6. Dynamic Anomaly Detection

Residuals are computed dynamically as e_t = y_t - y_hat_t. Telemetry points with standardized deviations exceeding 2.5σ are automatically flagged as severe anomalies. Across the dataset, 3 regional episodic spikes were detected and classified (stubble burning influx, Diwali fireworks, and localized thermal trapping episodes).
